3D - Invertibility and Isomorphisms

Invertible linear maps 

======================================

linear map T : V->W is invertible 

iff exists linear map S : W->V (inverse of T)

with ST = I : V->V ----- injective 
     TS = I : W->W ----- surjective

======================================

linear map T : V->W

T^(-1) := inverse of T is unique 

   S1          I 
v ----> w -----------> w  

   S1      T      S2  
v ----> w ---> v ----> w 

       I          S2  
v -----------> v ----> w 

======================================

linear map T : V->W is invertible 

iff T is injective & surjective

======================================

(has inverse)  

                  T      T^(-1)
 injectivity : v ---> w --------> !v 

                    T^(-1)        T
surjectivity : ∀w --------> ∃v ---> w

======================================

(T is bijection; define S: W->V)

          T        S         
    ∃!v ---> ∀w ---> ∃!v

(TS = I: W->W)

          T        S         T
    ∃!v ---> ∀w ---> ∃!v ---> w 

                        I   
              ∀w -------------> w 

    therefore TS = I (on W) by definition of S 

(ST = I: V->V)

    consider TS = I (on W)

         T      S         T 
    ∀v ---> w ---> (??) ---> w   

                          T 
                      !v ---> w

    therefore ST = I (on V) by injectivity of T

(S is linear : additivity)

    consider TS = I (on W)

             S               T
    w1 + w2 ---> S(w1 + w2) ---> w1 + w2

                             T
                 Sw1 + Sw2  ---> TSw1 + TSw2 = w1 + w2

    therefore S(w1 + w2) = Sw1 + Sw2 by injectivity of T

(S is linear : homogeneity)

    consider TS = I (on W)

        S          T
    aw ---> S(aw) ---> aw

                   T
            a(Sw) ---> a(TSw) = aw

    therefore S(aw) = a(Sw) by injectivity of T

======================================

dim V = dim W (finite dimensional)

linear map T: V->W 

then T invertible iff T injective 
                  iff T surjective 

because

dim V = dim null T  + dim range T --- Fundamental Theorem

  n   =     0       +      n
        (injective)   (surjective)

either alone gives bijection, hence invertible 

======================================

dim V = dim W (finite dimensional)

linear map S: W->V 
           T: V->W 

then ST = I (on V) iff TS = I (on W)

(in case of W = V, we have ST = TS = I)

because suppose ST = I (on V) 

   T      S
v ---> 0 ---> 0
  null   zero

      ST 
v ----------> v 
   identity

nullT = {0} --- T injective & surjective & invertible 

(ST)(T^(-1)) = (I)(T^(-1))
(S )         =    (T^(-1))

therefore TS = (T)(T^(-1)) = I
